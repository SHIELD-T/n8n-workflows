# DAY 87: Strategic Partnerships
**Week:** 13 — Advanced Business  |  **Time:** 2-3 hours  |  **Difficulty:** Advanced

## 🎯 What You'll Learn
- The real difference between a technology partnership, a channel/referral partnership, and a strategic alliance — and which one fits your business stage
- How to write a partnership proposal that leads with the partner's benefit, not yours
- Why a named, real prospective partner beats a list of partnership "categories"

Watch for any mention of tool integrations or ecosystem partners — that's the kind of relationship you're proposing today.

## 🛠️ Build It: Step-by-Step
1. List 5 real, named companies or individuals who could plausibly be a partner — not "CRM platforms" but "Airtable's partner program" or a specific named local business consultant you know of.
2. For each, classify the partnership type: technology/integration, channel/referral, or strategic alliance, and write one sentence on what's in it for them specifically (not for you).
3. Pick the single most realistic one — the one you could actually reach this month — and write a full one-page partnership proposal: what you're proposing, the value exchange (be concrete: "we refer clients needing X, you refer clients needing automation"), and a simple revenue-share or reciprocity structure.
4. Draft the actual first outreach message to this named partner — a real, sendable email or DM, addressed to a real name if you know one.
5. Import `WEEK_13_ADVANCED_BUSINESS/EXAMPLES/market_expansion_partnership_system.json` and compare its partnership-proposal structure to yours — note whether you're missing a success-metrics section or an onboarding/implementation step.
6. Write a 3-step "if they say yes" plan: first call agenda, what you'd need from them technically/contractually, and how you'd track whether the partnership is actually working after 60 days.

## 🔑 Credentials Needed
None required for this exercise.

## ✅ Definition of Done
- [ ] You have 5 real, named partnership candidates, not generic categories
- [ ] Your one chosen partner has a full one-page proposal with a concrete value exchange
- [ ] You have a real, sendable first-outreach draft addressed to this specific partner
- [ ] You have a 3-step follow-up plan for if they say yes, including a 60-day success check

## 🐛 Common Pitfalls
- **Partnership proposals that only benefit you** — if the value exchange section reads like "they send us clients," rewrite it around what you're giving them in return; one-sided proposals get ignored.
- **Naming a partnership "category" instead of a real prospect** — "reseller partnerships" isn't something you can send an email to; you need one specific name to actually test this.
- **No plan for measuring whether it worked** — a partnership with no defined success check will quietly die; the 60-day tracking step in this exercise is not optional.

## 🏭 Industry Track Application
Choose your named partner from your specific industry track (a real estate CRM vendor for a Real Estate track, a specific EHR consultant for HealthTech) so the reciprocity story is concrete rather than generic "we both serve businesses."
