## 🎯 What You'll Learn
- How to compress market expansion, partnerships, pricing, brand, and content into one strategy a person could actually act on this quarter
- How to tell the difference between a strategy document and a strategy you could start executing tomorrow
- How to pressure-test your own pricing against what you drafted in Week 12

## 🎥 Watch First
No new video today — this is a consolidation day for everything from Days 86-91.

## 🛠️ Build It: Step-by-Step
1. Gather what you produced on Days 86-91: your target market/niche, one partnership idea, your pricing tiers, your brand positioning statement, and your content plan.
2. Cut it to a single one-page "Next Quarter Plan": one market to pursue, one specific partner to reach out to (name a real company or person, not a category), your final 3-tier pricing with actual dollar amounts, one brand sentence, and 4 content pieces you'll actually publish.
3. Compare your pricing tiers against the ones in `WEEK_13_ADVANCED_BUSINESS/EXAMPLES/advanced_pricing_brand_system.json` and `WEEK_12_SCALING/EXAMPLES/aaas_service_management_system.json` — are yours consistent with what you scoped in Week 12, or did they drift? Reconcile any mismatch now.
4. Draft the actual first outreach message to the named partner from step 2 (not a placeholder — a real message you could send this week).
5. Pick one of your 4 planned content pieces and actually write it (a real blog post draft, LinkedIn post, or video script — not just a title in a content calendar).

## 🔑 Credentials Needed
None required for the review itself.

## ✅ Definition of Done
- [ ] You have a one-page Next Quarter Plan with real numbers and a named partner, not placeholders
- [ ] Your pricing matches (or you've explicitly reconciled it with) what you set in Week 12
- [ ] You have a real, sendable draft outreach message to the named partner
- [ ] You have one fully written content piece, not just a title on a calendar

## 🐛 Common Pitfalls
- **Strategy sprawl** — the source material this week covers 5+ areas; resist the urge to keep all of them equally detailed. A one-page plan with one real partner beats a ten-page plan with a partnership "category."
- **Pricing that quietly changed since Week 12** — it's easy for tiers to drift upward or downward as you write pricing language; check the actual numbers against your Week 12 EXAMPLES comparison, don't just trust memory.
- **Content calendar with no finished piece** — a calendar of titles proves planning, not execution. This week's Definition of Done requires one piece to actually exist.

## 🏭 Industry Track Application
Your named partner and content piece should both be specific to your industry track — a real company in that vertical, and a content piece that speaks to that vertical's actual buyers, not generic automation marketing copy.
