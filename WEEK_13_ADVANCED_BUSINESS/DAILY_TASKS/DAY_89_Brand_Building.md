# DAY 89: Brand Building
**Week:** 13 — Advanced Business  |  **Time:** 2-3 hours  |  **Difficulty:** Advanced

## 🎯 What You'll Learn
- How to write a positioning statement specific enough that it would actually be wrong for a competitor to use
- Why brand voice needs concrete examples ("write like X, not like Y"), not adjectives like "professional and approachable"
- How to check that a brand promise is one you can actually deliver on today, not an aspiration

## 🛠️ Build It: Step-by-Step
1. Write your positioning statement using this exact structure: "For [specific target customer], [your business] is the [category] that [key benefit] — unlike [specific alternative], we [real differentiator]." Fill in every bracket with something specific to your actual business, not a template placeholder.
2. Test it: read it aloud and ask whether a close competitor could honestly say the same sentence about themselves. If yes, sharpen the differentiator until it's actually yours.
3. Pick 3 real brand voice traits (not from a generic list) and for each, write one example sentence in that voice about your service, plus one "anti-example" sentence showing what your brand would NOT sound like.
4. Choose a real color palette and one real font pairing (2 fonts) — use an actual tool (Coolors, Google Fonts) to pick them, not placeholders — and write down the hex codes and font names.
5. Write your actual "About" page copy (150-250 words) using the positioning statement and voice from steps 1-3 — a real, publishable draft.
6. Import `WEEK_13_ADVANCED_BUSINESS/EXAMPLES/advanced_pricing_brand_system.json` and check: does your brand positioning connect logically to your Day 88 pricing (i.e. does a premium brand justify premium pricing, or vice versa)?

## ✅ Definition of Done
- [ ] Your positioning statement is specific enough that a direct competitor couldn't honestly claim the same sentence
- [ ] You have 3 voice traits each with a real example sentence and a contrasting anti-example
- [ ] You have real hex codes and named fonts, not placeholder descriptions
- [ ] You have a publishable About page draft, not bullet-point notes

## 🐛 Common Pitfalls
- **Positioning statements so generic they fit any automation business** — "we help businesses save time with automation" is true of every competitor too; the "unlike X, we Y" clause is what makes it yours, don't skip it.
- **Brand voice described only in adjectives** — "professional yet approachable" gives no one anything to write from; the example-sentence-plus-anti-example pairing in step 3 exists specifically to fix this.
- **Visual identity with no real assets** — describing a color palette in prose without picking actual hex codes means nothing gets built consistently; use a real tool and write down the real values.

## 🏭 Industry Track Application
Your positioning's "unlike [specific alternative]" clause should name a real alternative your industry track's buyers actually consider (a generic Zapier template, an in-house dev, a big consultancy) — not a vague "traditional methods."
