## 🎯 What You'll Learn
- How to turn Day 97's private "Automation Playbook" draft into something genuinely publishable and useful to someone else
- Why teaching what you learned is the single highest-leverage way to build a visible track record after a course ends
- How to package knowledge so it outlives this specific 15-week course structure

## 🛠️ Build It: Step-by-Step
1. Open your Day 97 Automation Playbook draft. Read it as if you were a stranger encountering it for the first time — mark anything that assumes context only you have.
2. Rewrite the 5 lessons-learned entries so each stands alone: situation, what went wrong or worked, the specific fix — with zero unexplained references to "this course" or "Day X."
3. Add one genuinely new section: "3 things I'd tell someone starting from zero" — written for a real beginner, not a course classmate.
4. Publish the playbook somewhere public (a blog post, a public Notion page, a LinkedIn article, a GitHub README) — the same bar as Day 101's portfolio: it needs a real, working public URL.
5. Pick your single best lesson from it and post it standalone on one platform where your target audience (clients or employers) would actually see it — a real post, published today, not scheduled for "later."
6. Add a line linking back to your Day 101 portfolio and Day 103 contact info, so anyone who finds the playbook can find you.
7. Import `WEEK_15_GRADUATION/EXAMPLES/graduation_achievement_system.json` and check if it references any legacy/knowledge-sharing pattern worth incorporating.

## 🔑 Credentials Needed
Whatever publishing platform you choose (blog, Notion, LinkedIn, GitHub) — free tier is sufficient.

## ✅ Definition of Done
- [ ] Your playbook is published at a real public URL, verified accessible outside your own login
- [ ] Every lesson entry is understandable without course-specific context
- [ ] You published at least one standalone post extracted from it today
- [ ] It links back to your portfolio and contact info

## 🐛 Common Pitfalls
- **A playbook that only makes sense to someone who took this exact course** — if a term or reference needs "Day 97" to be understood, rewrite it in plain language.
- **Treating "legacy" as a private document instead of something shared** — the value only exists once it's actually public and someone else can find and use it.
- **Publishing without linking back to how to reach you** — a genuinely useful public post with no path back to you wastes the credibility it builds.

## 🏭 Industry Track Application
Weight your published playbook toward your industry track's real problems — a HealthTech-focused lesson-learned post reaches the right audience far more effectively than generic automation advice competing with thousands of similar posts.
