# DAY 73: TUESDAY - Packaging Your Services
**Week:** 11 — Client Acquisition  |  **Time:** 2-3 hours  |  **Difficulty:** Intermediate

## 🎯 What You'll Learn
- The difference between one-off, retainer, and productized-template pricing models
- How to write a package around a client-facing outcome instead of a list of technical features
- How to set an actual dollar price you can defend, not a guess
- Why having 3 tiers (not 1) increases the odds a prospect picks something

## 🎥 Watch First
**Watch:** [N8N FULL COURSE 5 HOURS (Build & Automate Anything)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7WsbtZwOx_U) - Service packaging strategies section

Pay attention to: how pricing is tied to outcomes/time saved, not to "number of nodes" or hours worked — this is the mindset shift for today.

## 🛠️ Build It: Step-by-Step
1. Open a blank doc and write a 3-tier pricing table for your Day 72 niche, with exact dollar ranges — e.g., **Starter** ($500-800 one-off setup), **Growth** ($1,500-2,500 setup + $300-500/mo retainer), **Pro** ($3,000+ custom system + $800+/mo retainer).
2. For each tier, list 3-5 concrete deliverables (not vague — "automated lead capture from your website form into your CRM within 60 seconds," not "automation setup").
3. Write one sentence of value proposition per tier framed as an outcome ("saves ~10 hours/week of manual data entry"), not a feature list.
4. Define a timeline for each tier (e.g., Starter: 1 week; Growth: 2-3 weeks; Pro: 4+ weeks).
5. Write your payment terms for each tier (e.g., 50% upfront / 50% on delivery for one-offs; first month upfront for retainers).
6. Cross-check your pricing against what similar freelance automation/n8n services charge (search Upwork, Fiverr, or automation agency websites) and adjust if you're wildly out of range in either direction.
7. Save this pricing table — you'll paste it directly into tomorrow's portfolio and Day 76's proposal template.

## ✅ Definition of Done
- [ ] A 3-tier pricing table exists with specific dollar amounts (not "TBD" or "varies")
- [ ] Each tier has 3-5 concrete, outcome-framed deliverables
- [ ] Each tier has a timeline and payment terms
- [ ] You checked your pricing against at least 2-3 real market comparisons

## 🐛 Common Pitfalls
- Pricing purely by "hours worked" instead of value delivered — this caps your income and undersells automation's ROI
- Making tiers too similar in scope, so there's no clear reason to pick the higher one
- Forgetting to price the retainer separately from the setup — ongoing management is a distinct, recurring value

## 🏭 Industry Track Application
Package your industry automation skills into offerings tailored to your track's specific needs — e.g., a healthtech package might emphasize compliance-aware intake automation; a logistics package might emphasize delivery-exception alerting. Name each tier using language your niche's business owners would actually use, not generic "automation" jargon.
