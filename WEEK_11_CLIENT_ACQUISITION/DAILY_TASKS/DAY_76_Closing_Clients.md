# DAY 76: FRIDAY - Closing Clients
**Week:** 11 — Client Acquisition  |  **Time:** 2-3 hours  |  **Difficulty:** Intermediate

## 🎯 What You'll Learn
- How to structure a discovery call so you understand the client's real problem before pitching a solution
- How to write a proposal that maps directly to your Day 73 pricing tiers
- How to handle the 3 most common objections (price, timeline, "let me think about it")
- The difference between a proposal and a contract, and why you need both

## 🎥 Watch First
**Watch:** [n8n Beginner Course (1/9) - Introduction to Automation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4BVTkqbn_tY) - Closing automation clients section

Pay attention to: how the discovery-call structure moves from listening to proposing — note the ratio of questions asked vs. pitching done.

## 🛠️ Build It: Step-by-Step
1. Write a discovery-call script with 4 sections: opening/rapport, discovery questions (at least 5 specific questions about their current process), qualification (budget/timeline), and next steps.
2. Using your Day 73 pricing tiers, write one full proposal template with: executive summary, current-state problem, proposed solution, pricing breakdown, timeline, and terms.
3. Write a simple one-page contract template covering: parties, scope of work, payment terms, timeline/deliverables, and a termination clause — this doesn't need to be legally exhaustive, but it needs to exist before you take real money.
4. Write down the 3 objections you're most likely to hear ("too expensive," "I need to think about it," "can you do it faster/cheaper") and draft one calm, honest response to each — not a manipulative script, a genuine answer.
5. Role-play the full discovery call → proposal → close sequence out loud once, either with a classmate/friend or by recording yourself — this reveals awkward phrasing faster than reading silently.
6. If you got a reply from yesterday's outreach, use today's script for a real conversation. If not, practice the full sequence with a mock scenario based on your Day 72 niche.

## ✅ Definition of Done
- [ ] A discovery-call script with real, specific questions (not generic filler)
- [ ] A complete proposal template using your actual Day 73 pricing
- [ ] A one-page contract template covering scope, payment, timeline, and termination
- [ ] Written responses to your 3 most likely objections
- [ ] You practiced the full sequence out loud at least once

## 🐛 Common Pitfalls
- Pitching a solution before you've asked enough discovery questions — this leads to proposals that miss the real problem
- Treating objections as things to overcome by pressure rather than honest information exchange
- Skipping the contract because "it's just a small project" — even simple engagements benefit from a written scope

## 🏭 Industry Track Application
Master the art of closing automation clients in your industry with industry-specific proposals: reference the specific pain points and compliance/operational language your niche cares about (e.g., HIPAA-aware language for healthtech, SLA language for logistics) rather than a generic proposal template.
