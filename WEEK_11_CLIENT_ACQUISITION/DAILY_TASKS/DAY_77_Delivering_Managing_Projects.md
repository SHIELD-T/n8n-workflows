# DAY 77: SATURDAY - Delivering & Managing Projects
**Week:** 11 — Client Acquisition  |  **Time:** 3-4 hours  |  **Difficulty:** Intermediate

## 🎯 What You'll Learn
- How to structure a project into phases with clear deliverables per phase
- How to write a status update that keeps a non-technical client informed without overwhelming them
- What belongs in a pre-delivery checklist before you hand off automation to a client
- How to close out a project cleanly, including support terms

## 🎥 Watch First
**Watch:** [N8N FULL COURSE 5 HOURS (Build & Automate Anything)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7WsbtZwOx_U) - Project delivery and management section

Pay attention to: how the instructor separates "what the client sees" (status updates, deliverables) from "how you actually manage the work" (task tracking) — you need both, but they're different documents.

## 🛠️ Build It: Step-by-Step
1. Pick a free project-tracking tool you'll actually use (Notion, Trello, or even a structured Airtable base) and set up one project template with 3 phases: Discovery & Design, Build & Test, Deploy & Train.
2. For each phase, list specific tasks and deliverables (reuse your Day 76 proposal's scope as the source of truth).
3. Write a weekly status-update template with 5 sections: project overview, completed this week, planned next week, risks/issues, next milestone — fill in a mock example using your Day 72 niche as the fictional client.
4. Write a pre-delivery checklist (workflows tested, documentation complete, training materials ready, client approval obtained) and a post-delivery checklist (support period start date, monitoring active, feedback collected).
5. Write a short client-facing "handover" document template: what was built, how to access it, who to contact for support, and what happens after the support period ends.
6. Set up one real Airtable or Notion record using this template for either a real prospect (if you have one) or your Day 72 mock niche, so you have a working example, not just blank templates.

## 🔑 Credentials Needed
- Notion, Trello, or Airtable account (whichever you choose for tracking)

## ✅ Definition of Done
- [ ] A project template exists with 3 defined phases and tasks/deliverables per phase
- [ ] A filled-out example status update exists (not a blank template)
- [ ] Pre-delivery and post-delivery checklists are written
- [ ] A handover document template exists covering access, support contact, and support period end
- [ ] At least one real record was created in your tracking tool using this template

## 🐛 Common Pitfalls
- Using one tool/document for both internal task tracking and client-facing updates — clients don't need to see your messy internal task list
- Skipping the handover document — clients (and you, months later) need a record of what was delivered and how to get support
- Setting an indefinite, undefined support period — always specify an end date or a paid-retainer transition point

## 🏭 Industry Track Application
Build systems to deliver exceptional client experiences in your industry — the cadence and format of status updates should match what your niche's clients expect (a busy restaurant owner wants a 3-line text summary; a healthtech compliance officer may want a more formal written report).
