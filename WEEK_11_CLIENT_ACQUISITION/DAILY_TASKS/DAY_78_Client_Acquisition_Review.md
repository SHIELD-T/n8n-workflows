# DAY 78: Client Acquisition Review
**Week:** 11 — Client Acquisition  |  **Time:** 2-3 hours  |  **Difficulty:** Intermediate

## 🎯 What You'll Learn
- How to compress a week of scattered business assets (niche notes, pricing ideas, portfolio pieces, draft outreach) into one coherent, sendable package
- How to pressure-test a pitch by actually sending it, instead of leaving it in draft form
- How to separate "I have a document about this" from "I could hand this to a stranger today"

## 🎥 Watch First
No new video today — this is a consolidation day for everything from Days 71-77.

## 🛠️ Build It: Step-by-Step
1. Open whatever you produced on Days 71-77 (niche research, service packages, portfolio draft, outreach messages, proposal/contract templates) and lay it all in one folder or doc.
2. Cut it down to a single one-page "Client Acquisition Kit": your chosen niche, the specific pain point you solve, your 3-tier pricing (from Day 72), one portfolio piece with a real or realistic before/after number, and your outreach message template.
3. Reference `WEEK_11_CLIENT_ACQUISITION/EXAMPLES/client_acquisition_automation_system.json` for a working n8n automation you can point to as proof you can actually build what you're pitching — import it and confirm it still runs.
4. Send your outreach message to 3 real contacts (a former colleague, someone in your target niche, a warm connection) — not a hypothetical "I would send this to..." Real send, even if it's low-stakes.
5. Log every reply (or non-reply after 48 hours) in a simple tracker: contact, date sent, response, next action.

## 🔑 Credentials Needed
None required for the review itself. If you're sending outreach through n8n rather than manually, you'll need a Gmail or SMTP credential.

## ✅ Definition of Done
- [ ] You have one single-page Client Acquisition Kit, not a scattered set of notes
- [ ] You sent your outreach message to 3 real people, with proof (sent-mail screenshot or log)
- [ ] You imported and successfully re-ran `client_acquisition_automation_system.json`
- [ ] Your tracker has at least one row per contact with a next action, not just a "sent" status

## 🐛 Common Pitfalls
- **Polishing the kit instead of sending it** — a one-page kit with a typo that actually gets sent beats a perfect kit that never leaves your drafts folder. Set a hard stop time for editing.
- **Generic outreach that could go to anyone** — if your message doesn't name the specific pain point for that specific contact's business, it reads as spam. Rewrite the opening line per contact even if the rest is templated.
- **No follow-up plan** — most replies come after a follow-up, not the first message. Decide now (in your tracker) when you'll follow up if you hear nothing.

## 🏭 Industry Track Application
Your Client Acquisition Kit should be written for your chosen industry track specifically — a Fintech pitch and an EdTech pitch should not read like the same template with words swapped. Name the industry, the specific automation pain point, and use language a buyer in that industry actually uses.
