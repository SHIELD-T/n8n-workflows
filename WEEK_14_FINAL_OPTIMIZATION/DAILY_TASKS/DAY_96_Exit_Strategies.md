# DAY 96: Exit Strategies
**Week:** 14 — Final Optimization  |  **Time:** 2-3 hours  |  **Difficulty:** Advanced

## 🎯 What You'll Learn
- Why most automation-business exits are small acquisitions or management buyouts, not IPOs — and how to plan realistically for your actual scale
- How to estimate a real, defensible valuation range using your actual (or realistically projected) revenue, not generic industry multiples
- What "exit-ready" documentation actually looks like, and why it's the same documentation that makes your business easier to run today

## 🎥 Watch First
[n8n Beginner Course (1/9) - Introduction to Automation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4BVTkqbn_tY)
This is a business-planning day, not a technical one — watch with an eye toward what a buyer or acquirer would need to see to trust the technical foundation is solid.

## 🛠️ Build It: Step-by-Step
1. Estimate your business's real (or realistically projected) annual revenue and rough profit margin from Day 94's numbers.
2. Apply a real, conservative valuation method appropriate to your actual scale: for a small service business, that's typically 1-3x annual revenue or 3-5x annual profit — calculate both and write down the resulting range, not a generic "$2M-$5M" figure copied from nowhere.
3. Pick the single most realistic exit path for a business at your current stage — most likely a small strategic acquisition (a larger agency buys your client base) or a management/employee buyout — and write one paragraph on why it fits better than the alternatives (IPO, PE acquisition) at this stage.
4. Write a real "exit-readiness" checklist: financial records current, client contracts assignable, credentials documented and transferable, workflows documented (tie back to Day 79's templating and Day 99/95's QA checklist).
5. Run through the checklist honestly against your actual current state and mark what's real vs. still missing.
6. Pick the single biggest gap and take one concrete action to close it today (e.g. actually write down where every client credential lives, if that's currently only in your head).

## 🔑 Credentials Needed
None required — this is a planning and documentation exercise.

## ✅ Definition of Done
- [ ] Your valuation range is calculated from real/realistic revenue and profit numbers with the math shown
- [ ] You picked one realistic exit path for your actual current stage and can justify why
- [ ] You ran your exit-readiness checklist honestly against your real current state
- [ ] You closed at least one real gap the checklist surfaced today, not just noted it

## 🐛 Common Pitfalls
- **Valuation multiples copied from large-company M&A news instead of scaled to your actual size** — a $2M ARR business doesn't sell at the same multiple as a $50M one; use small-business-appropriate ranges.
- **Planning for an IPO or major PE acquisition at a stage where neither is realistic** — it's fine to dream big, but this exercise is more useful when grounded in your business's actual current scale.
- **Checklist filled out optimistically instead of honestly** — the entire value of this exercise is finding the real gap; marking everything "done" defeats the purpose.

## 🏭 Industry Track Application
For a Fintech/HealthTech track, your exit-readiness checklist must explicitly include compliance documentation (data handling agreements, audit trails) since a buyer in these spaces will refuse to proceed without them, regardless of how good the automation itself is.
