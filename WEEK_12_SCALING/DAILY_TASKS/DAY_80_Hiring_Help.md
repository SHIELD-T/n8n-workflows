# DAY 80: Hiring Help
**Week:** 12 — Scaling  |  **Time:** 2-3 hours  |  **Difficulty:** Advanced

## 🎯 What You'll Learn
- How to turn "I need help" into a specific, postable role with a defined boundary of what they will and won't touch
- The real difference between hiring a freelancer per-project vs. bringing on a recurring team member
- How to structure onboarding so a new hire can be productive in days, not weeks

## 🎥 Watch First
[What I Wish I Had Known Before Building 100+ n8n Workflows](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VB0ANci--Dc)
Listen for anything about delegation or what work got handed off as the business grew — that's today's real subject.

## 🛠️ Build It: Step-by-Step
1. List every recurring task in your automation business (from Week 11's business plan) that isn't strategic — client onboarding calls, workflow QA, support tickets, invoicing.
2. Pick the single task that eats the most of your week. That's your first hire's role.
3. Write a real job posting: title, 3-4 required skills, 3-4 responsibilities, a specific pay range or project rate, and whether it's freelance/contract or part-time.
4. Write a one-page "role card" for this hire: what they own end-to-end, what credentials/tools they'd need access to (be specific — "read access to the client Airtable base," not "our systems"), and what stays with you (billing, client strategy calls, credential ownership).
5. Draft a 3-question skills-screening test relevant to the role (e.g. for an n8n builder: "here's a broken workflow, fix it and explain what was wrong").
6. Import `WEEK_12_SCALING/EXAMPLES/team_management_hiring_system.json`, run it once, and compare its onboarding-week structure to a plan you sketch for your own first hire's first week.
7. Post the job listing somewhere real (Upwork, a Slack community, X/LinkedIn) if you're ready — otherwise save the draft as your ready-to-post posting.

## 🔑 Credentials Needed
None required for this exercise; whichever platform you post to (Upwork, LinkedIn) if you publish it.

## ✅ Definition of Done
- [ ] You have one specific role identified (not "someone to help with automation")
- [ ] You have a real, ready-to-post job listing with pay range and responsibilities
- [ ] Your role card explicitly names what the hire is NOT allowed to touch
- [ ] You have a 3-question skills screen you could actually use in a real interview

## 🐛 Common Pitfalls
- **Hiring plans with no defined boundary** — "they'll help with automation" isn't a role; if you can't say what they're explicitly not allowed to touch (client billing, master credentials), the role card isn't finished.
- **Pay range too vague to attract real applicants** — "competitive pay" filters out good candidates who want to know if it's worth applying; put a real number or range.
- **Screening test that doesn't test the actual job** — a generic n8n quiz doesn't tell you if someone can debug your specific kind of workflow; base it on a real (sanitized) problem you've hit.

## 🏭 Industry Track Application
If your industry track is Marketing automation, your first hire is probably a workflow QA/support person who can handle client campaign tweaks; for Fintech, prioritize someone who can be trusted with compliance-sensitive credential boundaries from day one.
